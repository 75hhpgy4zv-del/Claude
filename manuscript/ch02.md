# 2

The apartment had one good room and Derek had given it to the podcast.

Not the bedroom, which had a mattress and a lamp and a stack of library books that
constituted a moral failing. The second bedroom. It had the shelf, the projector, the
disc player he'd bought used off a man in Fontana who cried a little handing it over,
and a chair that cost more than his first car.

Wednesday, 10:12. Pre-flight.

Lamp on, other lamp off. Disc in the tray, even though he'd be streaming off a file,
because he wanted the disc in the building. Tea in the Drafthouse mug with the chipped
handle. Levels. Levels again. Phone face-down in the drawer, not on the desk — in the
drawer, closed.

Then two minutes in the chair with the camera off, doing nothing, coming down out of the
day. You cannot get on a microphone carrying fifth period. It goes into your voice as a
low hum and the hum is audible.

At 10:40 he went live.

---

"Hi. Hey. Okay."

Nine people. Then thirty-one, then fifty-something as the notification went out.

"So it's the first week of school, I have taught for three days, I have the personality
of a wrung-out sponge, and I decided at approximately 2 p.m. that this is going to be a
Verhoeven year." He drank the tea. "Selfishly. I need to spend the fall with a Dutchman
who thinks America is a lunatic asylum with good teeth."

*mothlight: LETS GOOOO*
*kaiju_dad: my wife has left the room*
*mothlight: tell her it's about labor*
*kaiju_dad: it IS about labor*

"It is about labor. Kaiju, you're doing my job and I resent it."

*PERSIMMON_77: sir. the disc.*

"The disc is in the tray. The disc is always in the tray. I don't justify the disc to
you people."

He ran *RoboCop* for eighty minutes without looking at his notes once. He did the ED-209
scene and what it means that the movie's funniest joke and its most upsetting image are
the same forty seconds of film. He read out, in full, the text of a 1985 Detroit city
council resolution he'd found in an afternoon he should have spent doing something else,
and then read the film's fake ad copy straight after it, and shut up, and let the room
sit in how little daylight there was between them.

Read the real thing aloud. Read the made-up thing aloud. Shut up. He'd been doing that
move for eleven years.

Eighty-eight people.

Somewhere in the second hour a name he half recognised asked if he'd seen the new one,
and he said he hadn't, and that he wanted to see it in a room with strangers, and that
he wouldn't have an opinion on it until Sunday.

*why sunday*

"Because there's an entire economy now that pays people to have the opinion first, and
I make four hundred dollars a month, and do you understand what that buys me? It buys me
the ability to be *late*. I'm the last man in America who can afford to still be
thinking about something on Sunday."

*mothlight: he's monologuing and he's right*
*kaiju_dad: four hundred dollars of pure freedom*

"Four hundred and eleven, and I'm proud of it."

*so is ant-man still off the table*

Derek put the mug down. "Ant-Man is not off the table. Ant-Man does not interact with
the table. In 2015 I said, on a podcast with eleven listeners, one of whom was my
mother, that I would never watch that film. About a movie I had no feelings about. On a
Tuesday. To nobody." He shrugged at ninety people. "Bits are for people with an
audience. In 2015 I had eleven listeners, so it can't be a bit. It can only be a
promise. And I keep those, because it's the one thing that's free."

*THE BIT*
*it's genuinely not a bit is the thing*

And then, in the middle of that, from the handle that had been there since 2019 and had
never once said anything about itself:

*PERSIMMON_77: can i ask something off topic*

"Sure."

*PERSIMMON_77: you've done this 11 years and i've never heard you mention a single
person in your life. not one. is that a rule or is there just nobody*

The room went strange. Not silent — chat doesn't do silent — but the scroll changed
character, people typing over each other and then stopping.

*oof*
*persimmon what the hell*
*mothlight: ok that's a real question though*
*PERSIMMON_77: sorry. genuine question. delete it if you want*

Derek heard himself take a breath into a live microphone, which he had told himself for
a decade never to do.

The honest answer was available to him immediately, the way honest answers always were,
sitting right there like a coat by the door. He could feel the shape of the dishonest
one too — *ha, mysterious, moving on* — and he could feel his mouth decline to make it,
the way it always declined, which was a thing about himself he had stopped arguing with
around thirty.

"It's a rule," he said. "And there's nobody."

*oh*

"Both. It's a rule *because* there's nobody, which is a more embarrassing answer than
the one you were fishing for." He turned the mug a quarter turn on the desk. "I was
married. That ended in 2021. I've got parents forty minutes away and a brother I don't
call back, and everybody else I know I know through this, through a screen, which
includes you, Persimmon, and I'm aware of how that sounds. So there's nothing to mention.
It's not discipline. There's just no list."

*derek*
*mothlight: 🫂*
*kaiju_dad: for what it's worth this is the best hour of my week man*
*PERSIMMON_77: i shouldn't have asked that on stream*
*we love you derek*
*honestly same though*

That was worse. He hadn't expected it to be worse.

"Okay, no, we're not doing this," he said, and his voice came out wrong, too fast, and
he heard that too. "I appreciate it and we're not doing it, because if we do it I'll cry
on a Wednesday and then I'll have to be a person about it on Sunday. Back to the movie.
Somebody ask me something about Kurtwood Smith."

*kaiju_dad: is kurtwood smith the greatest american screen villain*

"Thank you. Yes. Next question."

He got forty more minutes out of it and they were good, and he was funny, and none of it
sat right in his chest.

---

He signed off at 12:26 and did the outro badly, the way he did every week.

Then the light went out.

He sat in the expensive chair with his hands on his knees while his ears rang faintly
and the apartment came back around him. On the shelf behind him, in the dark, the
steelbooks stood in a row like a small municipal building.

Peak 94. Average 81. Four new subs, one gift sub, chat log saved by the script.

He opened the log, because he wanted to check whether he'd sounded as bad as he thought,
and scrolled to the timestamp, and read his own line in plain text with no voice on it.

*It's a rule because there's nobody.*

He looked at that for a while. Then he closed the laptop, rinsed the mug, put the disc
back in its case in the right place in the row.

Then he took his phone out of the drawer, where there were two messages from his brother
and one from his father, and read them standing in the dark kitchen with his thumb, and
the day did not end so much as stop.
