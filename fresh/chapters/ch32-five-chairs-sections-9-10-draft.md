# Chapter 32 — "Five Chairs"
## Sections 9–10 (draft v2)

*Picks up after Miru's "Hi, Gracie."*

---

Nobody had eaten.

Yeong-ju found this out from the catering crate by the door and announced it to the room.

"There's rice. There's soup. Why is there so much fruit."

"Nobody touches the fruit," said Nari.

Yeong-ju came over with a container of orange segments and stood in front of Derek's chair and looked at the problem, which was that both of his hands were in use.

She held one up.

"I'm fine."

"You flew from Los Angeles."

"I ate on the plane."

"Nobody eats on the plane," said Yeong-ju, and put it in his mouth.

---

"Does she eat," said So-hyun.

"Yes."

"What."

Derek thought about it. "Nothing hot."

Nobody said anything to that. Nari looked at So-hyun, and So-hyun did not look back.

---

Then the hand came out of his sleeve.

It came out blind, past his shoulder, palm up, and stopped in the air next to Yeong-ju, and stayed there.

Nobody moved.

"She wants one," said Derek.

Yeong-ju put a segment in it. The hand closed and went back into the sleeve. After a moment there was the sound, against his collarbone, of somebody eating an orange with her eyes shut.

"She asked," said Nari, in Korean, not to him.

"She does that."

"Not to us," said Nari.

The hand came out again. Yeong-ju filled it faster this time, and then stood there holding the container in both hands like a woman who has been given a job and does not intend to lose it.

---

"She had a rice cooker," said Yeong-ju.

Derek looked up.

"In the dorm. Under the bottom bunk."

"2012," said So-hyun.

"2013," said Nari.

"It was 2012, you weren't there yet."

"I heard about it."

"We ate it at two in the morning," said Yeong-ju. "Under the blankets." She shrugged one shoulder, the one without the container. "Cold. You couldn't heat anything."

Derek looked at the rice on the table, which had been sitting there since before the encore and was about room temperature.

He did not say anything.

"Before Mondays," said Miru, from the door.

"Before Mondays," Yeong-ju agreed, and nobody explained what that meant, and he did not ask.

---

"She eats on the floor," said Derek.

Four people turned around.

"At home. If there's a floor, she'll eat on it." He moved the hand under her knees about half an inch. "And she doesn't stir anything. If it needs stirring, you do it before it gets to her."

Nari put both hands over her mouth.

"She stirs mine," said So-hyun. She said it slowly, as though she were checking it. "In the van. Every time. She stirs mine and hands it back."

"Not at home," said Derek.

So-hyun looked at the back of Ha-eun's head for a while.

---

The hand came out. Yeong-ju had run out of orange. Derek said "rice," and Yeong-ju got the rice, and at about the fourth mouthful Ha-eun kicked him, twice, with one heel, on the shin.

"Smaller," said Derek.

"She *kicks* you," said Nari, delighted.

"Mostly she argues with me."

"About what?"

"Mangoes. It's my fault."

---

She went to sleep about twenty minutes later.

He knew before anybody could see it, because her hand let go of his shirt. The next hand that came out of the sleeve didn't finish the trip. It stopped on his knee, palm up, and stayed there.

Yeong-ju got a coat off the rail — long, black, SONDER across the back in white — and put it over both of them and tucked it in at Ha-eun's side, and then did not seem to know what to do next.

Nobody spoke.

After a while So-hyun said, at ordinary volume, "Ha-eun-ah."

Nothing.

So-hyun sat back.

---

It was very quiet after that, the way a room is quiet when everybody in it is being careful. Derek had been in a great many rooms with nothing to say, and in most of them he had asked somebody a question.

"How long have you been — " he said to Nari, and stopped, because he did not know how the sentence ended.

"Eleven years," said Nari.

"Right."

"In the group. Longer before."

"Right," said Derek.

Yeong-ju ate one of the orange segments herself, apparently without noticing.

"You were very good tonight," Derek said, to all of them, and heard it, and wished he hadn't.

"You were in row eleven," said So-hyun.

"I was."

"So you'd know."

"I would," said Derek.

---

It was Nari, eventually, in English, carefully.

"Why is she so thin?"

"That's hers," Derek said.

Nari looked at him. He looked back.

"You'll have to ask her."

Yeong-ju was looking at Ha-eun under the coat. She said something in Korean, very quietly, and it took Derek a second to get it, because it wasn't addressed to anybody in the room.

*Of course she didn't tell us.*

So-hyun turned her chair back around the right way and sat in it properly. She looked at her hands.

"Sorry. Before," she said. "The questions." And then: "We just needed to be sure that she was okay."

Derek nodded.

At the door, Miru looked down the corridor one more time. Then she closed it, and came across the room, and sat down in the chair with her name on it for the first time since she had come in.
