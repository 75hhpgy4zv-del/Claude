# Story Bible: *The Tender* (Book 3)

Set after *The Touge* (the frame novel) and after *50/50*. Pasted by the author in the working session;
the full text is kept in the session transcript. Summary of the major beats:

## Two movements
- **Part I, the troubled shoot.** Ha-eun (now an actor/producer with her own company) is executive producing a
  film, not *50/50*. The lead actress (20–21) falls for the male lead. Ha-eun protects their privacy because of
  her own history. The relationship turns volatile, the actress is visibly injured, and the disruptions pile up.
  Ha-eun won't intervene: "I wanted to give them what I never had." Her empathy becomes projection, and the cost
  moves onto the crew. The film (good work) is eventually shelved from accumulated instability.
- **Derek's role.** Ha-eun invites his judgment. He warns her ("If enough things are disrupted, nothing is safe").
  She gradually narrows his access: no dailies, producer-only meetings, rewrites sent elsewhere. Each step is
  defensible on its own. "You asked me what I thought. I answered you. And because my answer made you feel unsafe,
  you changed the terms of my access until I was outside." He quits working for her, then leaves the marriage.
  **The midpoint is his departure.**
- **Part II, India.** Six-plus months of silence. Derek lives in a shack on his family's land in the town where he
  spent part of his childhood, at a Five the whole time. He abandons the Levels (a report for someone else) for
  something more like color. He teaches English at the school named for his grandfather.
- **The third novel.** A man left on his wedding day; a failed, unheroic suicide attempt; travel; he returns home
  to Thailand and becomes a farmer; it ends with repairing land after rain. Ha-eun finds out when it's released
  (a lost privilege, not a violated right). She recognizes the place in the author photo.
- **Ha-eun goes, uninvited.** If he tells her to go home, she goes. She hears him speaking Telugu and laughing
  before she sees him. "My wife."
- **Two weeks together.** Culture shock: not knowing with no consequences. Cows (she names them), water, milk, the
  card game where cheating is part of the game, getting lost. Trust regrows through ordinary shared judgment.
- **The beach.** "I'm ready, you know, whenever you are." / "To go home." / "We don't have to."
- **The land.** Derek asks his father for land. "Okay. I have saved some for you."

## Themes
- What people owe one another when obligations conflict. Obligation without ownership.
- Ha-eun: power creates obligations even when you hate power. Understanding someone's pain doesn't erase the cost of their choices.
- Derek: an external locus of control. "Everyone understands you until it becomes a problem." India offers a
  *loving* jury, but a jury is still a jury. The grandfather never learned the school and church were named for
  him; some of the most important things about a life aren't available to the person living it.
- Class and wealth: Derek's simplicity is chosen, and the town's affection is inherited.
- His mother's shame kept him from finding out that his values had a lineage in the family.
