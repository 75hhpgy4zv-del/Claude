# Marisol "Mari" Ocampo — `marimint`

**Age** 29 · **From** Scarborough, Ontario · **Lives** Los Angeles (Sherman Oaks, rented,
four years, still hasn't hung anything) · **Followers** 6.4M across platforms ·
**Online since** 20 (nine years)

## Personality (the load-bearing part)

Modeled on the Pokimane archetype: personality only, no biography. See `decisions.md` D2.

- **Warmth as craft.** She is genuinely kind and she has also spent nine years making
  kindness reproducible on demand. She can no longer reliably locate the seam. This
  terrifies her more than any hate campaign.
- **Deflective comedy.** The joke lands one beat before the feeling would have. In
  private, with three people, the beat sometimes passes and the feeling arrives.
- **Boundary-literate.** She has read more about parasocial dynamics than her critics
  have. She says no cleanly, often, and early — and gets called *calculated* for the
  same behavior that would be called *professional* in a man.
- **The big sister of the internet.** Millions are sincerely fond of her; they
  sincerely believe they know her. She won't say the second part is false, because
  she thinks saying so would be a betrayal of the first part, which is real.
- **Business-fluent, quietly.** She built a company. Says "we" about it and lets
  people assume she means a team she works for.
- **Terrible at endings.** Streams, calls, arguments. Will keep talking to avoid the
  door.

## Wound

Legible to millions, known by four. No longer sure she'd survive a fifth.

## Wants

- **Stated:** stability, a slate of projects, to stop being surprised by her own life.
- **Actual:** to be boring in front of one person and have them stay.

## Voice

Fast, low, sentence fragments, self-interrupting. Swears more off-stream. Apologizes
reflexively then catches it and stops mid-word. Uses "like" as a rhythm unit, never
as a filler of uncertainty. When she is genuinely upset she becomes *more* articulate,
not less, and that's the tell.

## Physical

Small, restless hands. Sits with a foot tucked under her. Two versions of her face —
the one for the camera, thirty degrees more open, and the one at rest, which strangers
read as unfriendly and which is simply unemployed.
