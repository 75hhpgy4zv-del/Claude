# Chapter Thirteen
## Bias

Music Bank was the Thursday and she flew out Tuesday night.

Four people spent nine days arranging it and it cost the production about eleven thousand dollars in schedule, and she apologised for it twice, badly, to Mags, who said "it's three days, I've lost a lead to a *hernia*," and never mentioned it again.

She had been awake twenty-two hours by the time they got her to KBS. Live band, which she had asked for, which had been a mistake, which she would do again. Two costume changes. A camera rehearsal at four and a real one at six and a hold at seven-forty because somebody's in-ears had gone.

At 7:52 they walked her from the dressing rooms to the stage-left corridor, and coming the other way, off, in the yellow, were NewJeans.

She had about nine feet of warning and she used them badly.

What she should have done — what she had done ten thousand times in nine years, in this exact corridor, in worse states than this — was the wave. What she did instead was look at the floor.

She was aware of doing it while she did it. She watched herself, from somewhere behind her own eyes, deliberately look at the floor of a corridor at KBS in order to avoid a twenty-year-old in a yellow costume, and understood exactly why she was doing it, and could not stop.

They passed. It was fine. It was over.

Then somebody said, "Ha-eun-sunbaenim—"

Danielle had got about two feet past and turned round and come back.

She had her hands slightly out in front of her, not quite reaching, the way you do when you are not sure whether you are allowed, and she was pink, and she was talking before she had finished arriving.

"I'm so sorry, I know you're going on — I just, I have to, because I'm going to be annoyed with myself for a *year* if I don't."

"That's okay," Ha-eun said.

"I've loved you since I was *fifteen.*"

---

It landed somewhere under her ribs and did not do what it was supposed to do.

Nine years. Nine years of that sentence, in nine languages, at fansigns and airports and hospital visits, and she had a *system* for it — the hand on the arm, the eye contact held one beat past comfortable, the specific question back so the person had something to take home. She had done it forty times that week. She could do it with a fever.

And standing in a corridor with somebody's actual face in front of her, what her brain produced, in English, in Derek's voice, at full volume, was:

*forty minutes.*

"Thank you so much," said Ha-eun.

---

It came out flat.

She heard it come out flat. She heard the four most professionally deployed words of her adult life arrive in a corridor with nothing behind them at all, and she saw — because she was looking now, because it was too late — Danielle's face do the small recalibration of a person who has walked into something and does not know what.

And then Danielle carried on being lovely, which was the worst thing that happened to Ha-eun that year.

"The 2021 comeback got me through my audition period," she said, faster, pinker. "Genuinely. I had it on a *loop*, my mum still — sorry. Sorry, you're going on."

"That's very kind."

"You're — is it live tonight? With the band?"

"Yes."

"*Oh*, that's so cool," said Danielle, with her whole chest.

---

Ha-eun bowed.

She bowed at the correct angle. She could feel herself going to the manual for it — the way you go to the manual when the improvisation has stopped being available — and the angle was right, and the timing was right, and it was the single most correct bow of her professional life and there was not one thing in it.

"Thank you," she said again. "Good luck with everything."

*Good luck with everything.* To a woman with four hundred million streams.

She turned and walked to the stage-left mark and stood on it with her hands at her sides and did not look back down the corridor, and behind her, quite clearly, she heard Hyein say something and Danielle say *what?* and one of them laugh.
