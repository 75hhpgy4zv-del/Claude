# Novel idea — working notes

## Premise (layer one)
- Derek, an English teacher, wrote a novel, *80/20*, to get through his divorce.
- *80/20* is about an English teacher and a very famous streamer, Maya, who meet and
  fall in love. It's about how hard it is to open up and show your real self to someone.
- Ha-eun, lead singer of one of the top three K-pop groups in the world, connects
  deeply with Maya. She options the novel so she can produce the film and star in it.
- Sparks fly while they talk about the adaptation, and Derek and Ha-eun start falling in love.
- **The recursion on set:** the film recreates the book, which is itself Derek's projection
  of a love he hoped was still possible after the divorce shattered him. Now he's falling
  in love with a real woman who closely mirrors the one he imagined. The love feeds the
  performance, and the performance makes it easier for them to fall further in love.
- The set gives their time together a purpose, so nobody looks at it twice.
- Once the project wraps, they have no excuse to see each other, so they sneak around.
- They're caught and photographed, and people start guessing about the relationship.
- Derek is suspended from teaching and doxxed. The internet uses his book and his podcast
  to paint him as a predator.
- He hits rock bottom and finally writes the sequel, *50/50*, built on the experience of
  loving Ha-eun. It's even better than *80/20*.
- **Ending:** the premiere of the *80/20* film. Derek is on the red carpet being interviewed
  about the movie and the success of *50/50*. Ha-eun finds them, interrupts to gush about
  *50/50* herself, and teases that she has optioned it too: "get ready for the sequel,
  it's even bigger than the first one." One meta layer covers both the sequel novel and
  the sequel film.

## Theme (layer two)
Every character has a public image and a private reality. The space between those two
is what the novel is about.

### Ha-eun / Gracie
- Ha-eun has lost sight of who she really is. *80/20* shows her someone going through
  the same identity crisis, and she needs to know whether the story can come true for
  her the way it did for Maya.
- She has a second self she never shows: innocent, naive, childlike in some ways. This
  self can't live in the real world as an adult, because Ha-eun has been sheltered inside
  the idol system since before her teens. She has never done laundry or bought a banana
  at a grocery store, and she can't cook or clean.
- Derek names this version of her **Gracie**, after the character in Ha-eun's name that
  means "grace." Gracie becomes the way Ha-eun reclaims her identity.

### Derek's tattoos
- Derek has 12 tattoos that work like a moral map of his personality.
- One morning in bed, before they make love, Ha-eun traces them and Derek explains most of them.
- One is a quote: **"I would rather be given the cruel truth than fed gentle lies."**

### The scandal and the band
- When the scandal breaks, Ha-eun's company pressures her to recant and discredit Derek
  as a predator who took advantage of her.
- The tattoo quote has stayed with her. She can't bear to lie about Derek, so she refuses.
- The company shelves the band's comeback.
- Distraught, Ha-eun goes to the band to apologize and beg for forgiveness.
- They tell her they never planned to renew. They've each been quietly building a
  personal life, and they want to be authentic too: two members have been secretly
  dating each other, and another is married to someone in the military. She was
  protecting nothing, because they had all outgrown the life they were living.
