# 2

The apartment had one good room and Derek had given it to the podcast.

Not the bedroom, which had a mattress and a lamp and a stack of library books that
constituted a moral failing. The second bedroom, the one a normal thirty-five-year-old
would have made into an office or given to a child. It held the shelf — four hundred and
some discs, alphabetical inside chronological inside a system that had made sense in 2018
— and the projector, and a disc player he'd bought used off a man in Fontana who had
carried it out to the car himself, wrapped in a bath towel, and then stood in his own
driveway with his hands on his hips for a while before going back inside. There was a
chair in there that had cost more than Derek's first car. There was a rug he'd got for
the acoustics and a moving blanket nailed over the closet door for the same
reason, which killed a slap-back off the wall that nobody but him had ever heard.

Wednesday, 10:12. Pre-flight.

Lamp on, other lamp off. Disc in the tray, even though he'd be streaming off a file,
because he wanted the disc in the building. Tea in the Drafthouse mug with the chipped
handle. Levels. Levels again — a knuckle-rap on the desk, watching the meter jump green
and settle, the way he had done it four hundred and some Wednesdays running. Phone
face-down in the drawer, not on the desk. In the drawer, closed.

Then two minutes in the chair with the camera off, doing nothing. The room ticking. The
window behind the monitor going from blue to nothing while the refrigerator cycled in the
next room. You cannot get on a microphone carrying fifth period; it comes through as a
low hum under the voice and the hum is audible.

At 10:40 he went live.

---

"Hi. Hey. Okay."

Nine people. Then thirty-one, then fifty-something as the notification went out.

"So it's the first week of school, I have taught for three days, I have the personality
of a wrung-out sponge, and I decided at approximately two this afternoon that this is
going to be a Verhoeven year." He drank the tea. "Selfishly. I need to spend the fall
with a Dutchman who thinks America is a lunatic asylum with good teeth."

*mothlight: LETS GOOOO*
*kaiju_dad: my wife has left the room*
*mothlight: tell her it's about labor*
*kaiju_dad: it IS about labor*

"It is about labor. Kaiju, you're doing my job and I resent it."

*PERSIMMON_77: sir. the disc.*

"The disc is in the tray. The disc is always in the tray. I don't justify the disc to you
people."

He ran *RoboCop* for eighty minutes and never once looked at the notes. He did the ED-209
sequence, and what it means that the funniest joke in the film and its most upsetting
image are the same forty seconds of footage. He did the resurrection scene shot by shot,
leaning in until he was too close to the mic and hearing it and not caring, one hand
going while the other stayed flat on the desk. Then he read out, in full, the text of a
1985 Detroit city council resolution he had found in an afternoon he should have spent
doing something else — all of it, the whereases, the flat municipal language of a city
selling itself — and read the film's fake ad copy straight after it in exactly the same
voice, and stopped, and let the room sit in how little daylight there was between the
two.

Read the real thing aloud. Read the made-up thing aloud. Shut up. He had been doing that
move for eleven years.

Eighty-eight people.

Somewhere in the second hour a name he half recognised asked whether he'd seen the new
one, and he said he hadn't, and that he wanted to see it in a room with strangers, and
that he wouldn't have an opinion on it until Sunday.

*why sunday*

"Because there's an entire economy now that pays people to have the opinion first, and I
make four hundred dollars a month, and do you understand what that buys me? It buys me
the ability to be *late*. I'm the last man in America who can afford to still be thinking
about something on Sunday."

*mothlight: he's monologuing and he's right*
*kaiju_dad: four hundred dollars of pure freedom*

"Four hundred and eleven, and I'm proud of it."

*so is ant-man still off the table*

Derek put the mug down. "Ant-Man is not off the table. Ant-Man does not interact with the
table. In 2015 I said, on a podcast with eleven listeners, one of whom was my mother, that
I would never watch that film. About a movie I had no feelings about. On a Tuesday. To
nobody." He shrugged at ninety people. "Bits are for people with an audience. In 2015 I
had eleven listeners, so it can't be a bit. It can only be a promise. And I keep those,
because it's the one thing that's free."

*THE BIT*
*it's genuinely not a bit is the thing*

And then, from the handle that had been there since 2019 and had never once said anything
about itself:

*PERSIMMON_77: can i ask something off topic*

"Sure."

*PERSIMMON_77: you've done this 11 years and i've never heard you mention a single person
in your life. not one. is that a rule or is there just nobody*

The scroll changed character. Chat does not go silent, but it does something adjacent —
people starting and stopping, three lines coming at once and then a gap of four seconds
that in that room was enormous.

*oof*
*persimmon what the hell*
*mothlight: ok that's a real question though*
*PERSIMMON_77: sorry. genuine question. delete it if you want*

Derek heard himself take a breath into a live microphone, which he had told himself for a
decade never to do.

The honest answer was already there. It always was. He could feel the other one too, the
easy one — *ha, mysterious, moving on* — sitting just as close and costing nothing. His
mouth declined to pick it up. It always declined, and he had stopped arguing with that
somewhere around thirty.

"It's a rule," he said. "And there's nobody."

*oh*

"Both. It's a rule *because* there's nobody, which is a more embarrassing answer than the
one you were fishing for." He turned the mug a quarter-turn on the desk. "I was married.
That ended in 2021. There's parents forty minutes from here and a brother I don't call
back, and everybody else I know I know through this — through a screen — which includes
all of you, and I'm aware of how that sounds. So there's nothing to mention. It isn't
discipline. There's just no list."

*derek*
*mothlight: 🫂*
*kaiju_dad: for what it's worth this is the best hour of my week man*
*PERSIMMON_77: i shouldn't have asked that on stream*
*we love you derek*
*honestly same though*

That was worse. He had not expected it to be worse.

"Okay, no, we're not doing this." His voice came out too fast, and he heard that too.
"I appreciate it and we're not doing it, because if we do it I'll cry on a Wednesday and
then I'll have to be a person about it on Sunday. Back to the movie. Somebody ask me
something about Kurtwood Smith."

*kaiju_dad: is kurtwood smith the greatest american screen villain*

"Thank you. Yes. Next question."

He got another forty minutes and they were good and he was funny, and none of it sat
right in his chest.

---

He signed off at 12:26 and did the outro badly, the way he did every week.

Then the light went out and the room came back around him: the moving blanket, the fan of
the projector spinning down, the shelf standing in the dark behind him with the
steelbooks in their row like a small municipal building. His ears rang faintly. The
apartment above him was silent. Outside, somewhere down the block, a car alarm went off
and stopped on its own.

Peak 94. Average 81. Four new subs, one gift sub, the chat log saved out by the script.

He opened the log, because he wanted to know whether he'd sounded as bad as he thought,
and scrolled to the timestamp, and there it was in plain text with no voice on it and no
room around it.

*It's a rule because there's nobody.*

He looked at that for a while.

Then he closed the laptop and rinsed the mug and put the disc back in its case and the
case back in the row, in the right place, and stood in the doorway with his hand on the
switch a second longer than the room required.

Then he took the phone out of the drawer, where there were two messages from his brother
and one from his father, and read them in the dark kitchen with his thumb, and the day
did not end so much as stop.
