## Chapter 1: The Announcement

The email arrived at 9:47 a.m. on a Tuesday.

It was addressed to all cast and senior crew of *Enemies with Benefits*, CC'd to the GMMTV production office, the GMM 25 scheduling department, and someone in Legal whose name JingJing didn't recognize. The subject line read: **EWB SPECIAL EPISODE — PRODUCTION GREENLIGHT & EMBEDDED PRESS NOTIFICATION.**

JingJing read it on her phone, sitting cross-legged on Jan's couch, wearing Jan's oversized Sizzy reunion tour t-shirt and nothing else. Noo-Noo was in her lap. Her hair was still wet from the shower — the shower she had taken alone, because Jan was in the kitchen making Thai tea and had resisted all provocation, which meant JingJing had spent ten minutes under hot water thinking about Jan's hands instead of having Jan's hands, which was *worse*.

She read it again.

"P'Jan."

"Hmm."

"Come look at this."

Jan appeared in the doorway holding two glasses of Thai tea, the orange so vivid it looked irradiated. Grey joggers. White tank top. Hair tied up in the messy bun she wore at home, the one that bared the clean line of her jaw and the small mole below her left ear that JingJing had spent considerable time with her mouth on.

"What?" Jan said.

JingJing held up the phone.

Jan set both glasses down on the side table — carefully, because JingJing had yelled at her twice about water rings on the wood, which was rich coming from someone who left boba cups on every horizontal surface like a trail of evidence — and took the phone. JingJing watched her read. Jan's face when she was concentrating: the slight furrow, the way her lips parted, the unconscious tilt of her head toward the right. It made JingJing want to climb her. Most things about Jan made JingJing want to climb her. This was a pre-existing condition.

"*Married to My Enemy*," Jan read aloud. "Special episode. Wedding arc. Lal and Wine." A pause. "Embedded documentary crew. Journalist assigned to cover the production process from pre-production through broadcast."

"Keep reading."

"The journalist is — Sirin Atthayakorn. She's doing a feature piece for —" Jan looked up. "I don't know who that is."

"She wrote that thing about GL actresses and fan performance. 'The Ship and the Shore.'"

"I didn't read it."

"It was good. It was annoyingly good. She's smart."

"Okay."

"She's going to be on set. Every day. For the entire production."

"Okay."

JingJing stared at her. Jan stared back with the patient, faintly amused expression she wore when she was waiting for JingJing to arrive at a point she'd already seen coming.

"P'Jan," JingJing said slowly. "We are *engaged*."

"I'm aware."

"We are engaged, and there is going to be a journalist on set, and we are going to be filming a *wedding show*, and —"

Jan sat down next to her. Close. Their thighs pressed together. Jan's hand found the inside of JingJing's knee — casually, automatically, the way Jan always touched her, like the boundary between their bodies was an administrative fiction.

"And we'll be fine," Jan said.

"I know we'll be fine. I'm not worried."

"You sound worried."

"I don't worry. I'm not a worrier. I'm a problem identifier."

Jan's thumb drew a slow circle on the inside of her knee. "And the problem is?"

JingJing opened her mouth. Closed it. She looked down at Jan's hand, then at the ring on Jan's finger — simple white gold, no stone, because Jan's aesthetic was clean lines and quiet authority — and then at her own ring on the chain around her neck, the diamond warm against her sternum where it lived now, tucked between her heartbeat and the world.

"There is no problem," JingJing said.

"Correct."

"We just won't do anything stupid."

"We never do anything stupid."

This was a lie. They had done something stupid in the dressing room. They had done something stupid under the craft services table. They had done something stupid in a hallway, on a balcony, and against the wall of a hotel bathroom in Chiang Mai with the window open. "Stupid" was the load-bearing wall of their entire relationship, and removing it would collapse the structure.

But JingJing didn't say that. Because Jan's hand was moving higher — past the knee, along the inner thigh, fingertips tracing the hem of the t-shirt that had ridden up — and JingJing's brain was doing the thing it did when Jan touched her there, which was to abandon all other functions and focus entirely on whether Jan was going to keep going.

Jan stopped.

"We should call Prae," Jan said, withdrawing her hand like she hadn't just been two inches from changing the entire trajectory of the morning. "Find out the production timeline."

JingJing made a noise. It was not a word. It was the sound a kettle makes when you turn off the heat one degree before boiling.

Jan smiled. Picked up her Thai tea. Drank.

"I hate you," JingJing said.

"You love me."

"I love you and I hate you and those are the same thing and you know it."

"I do know it." Jan leaned over and pressed her lips to JingJing's forehead. Slow. Warm. Deliberate. JingJing's eyes closed. Every inch of brat drained out of her, and what was left was the thing underneath — the part of her that curled into Jan's chest and held Noo-Noo and needed nothing except this for the rest of her life.

"We're going to be fine," Jan said against her hairline.

"I know."

"I'll take care of it."

"I *know*."

"Good girl."

JingJing melted. Those two words in Jan's low voice did something to her nervous system that eight years of formal education hadn't equipped her to explain. She pressed her face into Jan's shoulder. Noo-Noo was squished between them. Jan's hand came up to the back of her head, fingers threading through wet hair, and for a moment the email and the journalist and the special episode didn't exist.

Then Jan's phone rang.

"That's Prae," Jan said, checking the screen. She answered with one hand, the other still in JingJing's hair. "Sawadee kha, Khun Prae. Yes, I saw it. Yes, she read it too. She's right here."

JingJing could hear Prae's voice, tinny and precise, laying out the timeline: pre-production starting next week, table reads the following Monday, costume fittings Wednesday, the journalist arriving Thursday. Sirin Atthayakorn. Three months on set. Full access.

Jan's hand kept moving in JingJing's hair. Slow strokes.

"Understood," Jan said. "We'll be professional." A pause. "Khun Prae, when have we ever been anything but professional?"

JingJing — who was currently wearing no underwear, sitting in Jan's lap, with a hickey on her inner thigh from last night — said nothing.

The production office for *Married to My Enemy* was the same one they'd used for the original run — a converted floor of the GMMTV building, all glass partitions and rolling whiteboards and the persistent smell of someone microwaving pad kra pao. The same desks, the same coffee machine that sounded like a dying animal, the same wall planner now blank and waiting for the new schedule.

Fah was the first person they saw.

She was standing by the elevator when the doors opened, holding a clipboard and wearing the expression of someone who had been awake since 5 a.m. Her lanyard said SENIOR PA in letters that were slightly too large, as if she'd printed them herself to make a point.

"P'Jan. P'JingJing." Her eyes swept them — the way they were standing (too close), Jan's hand (on JingJing's lower back, possessive and unconscious), JingJing (leaning into Jan like she needed the contact to remain upright).

"You need to not do that," Fah said.

"Do what?" Jan said.

Fah pointed at Jan's hand.

Jan looked down. She genuinely hadn't noticed. This was the problem — not the journalist, not the documentary, but the fact that Jan touched JingJing the way other people breathed: constantly, unconsciously, as a baseline condition of being alive.

"It's just my hand," Jan said.

"It's your hand on her *back*, P'Jan, and the journalist is here *Thursday*—"

"That's three days from now."

"Three days is *nothing*. Three days is — do you know how many things I have to do in three days? I have to brief the crew. I have to set up information-control protocols. I have to create a *perimeter*—"

"Fah," JingJing said gently. "Breathe."

Fah breathed. It did not appear to help.

"You two," Fah said, dropping her voice to the register she used when she was trying to be respectful to her seniors while also wanting to strangle them, "are the *worst-kept secret* in this building. Everyone knows. The camera team knows. The lighting crew knows. The caterers know. The caterers, P'JingJing. The people who bring the *food* know that you're together because you feed each other off the same spoon at craft services like you're in a kdrama."

"It was one time," JingJing said.

"It was eleven times. I counted."

Jan's hand had not moved from JingJing's back.

"You need to understand," Fah continued, pressing the clipboard to her chest, "that this journalist — this Sirin — she is going to be *everywhere*. On set. In the office. At fittings. At rehearsals. Full access. And she is—" Fah pulled out her phone, scrolled to something she'd bookmarked. "I read her work last night. Both pieces. She doesn't write gossip, P'Jan. She writes about body language. She writes about the way performers touch each other when they think no one is watching. She cross-references Instagram posting times to track co-location patterns. In 'The Ship and the Shore' she identified a couple from *schedule overlaps and social media timestamps* before anyone had confirmed it publicly."

JingJing felt something cold settle in her stomach. Not fear exactly. More like the distant recognition that a very smart person was about to walk into a room where she and Jan were constitutionally incapable of behaving.

Jan's face didn't change. "She sounds thorough."

"She sounds like she's going to figure you out in *forty-eight hours*." Fah's knuckles were white on the clipboard. "I need you to take this seriously. I need you to stop touching each other in public spaces. I need you to arrive in separate cars. I need you to—"

"Fah."

"—stop looking at each other like that."

"Like what?"

"Like *that*. Like what you're doing right now. Like she's the only other person in the room. P'Jan, you pushed her hair behind her ear during the last table read. In front of cameras. You reached across the table and did it and you didn't even *notice*."

The elevator chimed behind them. Lin walked out, phone in one hand, iced coffee in the other.

Lin looked at Jan. Looked at JingJing. Looked at the space between their shoulders — or rather, the absence of space. Looked at Fah's face.

"She saw the email," Lin said.

"She saw the email," Fah confirmed.

Lin took a long drink of her iced coffee. "How bad?"

"Journalist. Full access. Three months. She's the one who wrote 'The Ship and the Shore.'"

Lin's coffee stopped halfway to her mouth. She knew the piece. Every manager in the GL industry knew the piece — it had sent three PR teams into emergency meetings when it published, because Sirin had mapped relationship indicators from public data that teams had assumed were invisible. Posting time correlations. Arrival patterns at events. The specific way one actress's hand lingered on another's waist in a photo that was supposed to look casual.

"That Sirin?" Lin said.

"That Sirin."

Lin set her coffee down. She looked at Jan and JingJing. They were standing shoulder to shoulder again. Jan's pinky was hooked around JingJing's pinky. Neither of them appeared to be aware of this.

"Are you *aware* that this is a situation?" Lin asked.

"Of course we're aware," JingJing said.

"You're holding hands."

JingJing looked down. "That's a pinky. That's not a hand. That's one-fifth of a hand."

"JingJing."

"It's fine, P'Lin. We'll be discreet."

Lin looked at Fah. Fah looked at Lin. The look lasted two seconds and covered every near-miss of the past four months — every dressing room lock, every hallway close call, every time Lin had rounded a corner and seen something she had to immediately un-see. Lin thought of the Chiang Mai balcony. The sound through the hotel wall. The two figures she'd stood watching for longer than she'd ever admit.

"I need a drink," Lin said.

"It's ten in the morning," Fah said.

"I need a drink at ten in the morning."

JingJing's phone buzzed. She checked it, and her face lit — the full-beam glow that Fah privately called "the lighthouse," the one that came on whenever a specific name appeared on her screen.

"Love says congratulations on the special," JingJing said, typing back. "She and Milk are on set today too — Ditto table read." She looked at Jan. "We should go say hi. Milk said she wants to show you something about the motorcycle sequence."

"After we check in with Prae," Jan said.

"Obviously after. I'm not an animal."

"You ate cereal out of the box with your hands this morning."

"That's efficiency, not barbarism."

They drifted toward the production office, gravitating toward each other the way they always did — Jan's hand finding JingJing's lower back when they turned the corner, JingJing leaning into the touch without a thought.

Behind them, Fah and Lin stood in the hallway.

"Three months," Lin said.

"Three months."

"With a journalist who tracks Instagram posting times."

Fah said nothing. She opened her phone and pulled up the group chat she'd created at 2 a.m. after reading the email — three members: herself, Lin, and Prae. She'd named it "Operation: Keep These Idiots Alive."

**Fah:** They're here. They walked in holding pinkies. Jan's hand was on her back for the entire elevator ride. JingJing is wearing Jan's shirt.

**Prae:** Of course she is.

**Lin:** Has anyone actually read this woman's full body of work? Not just the GL pieces?

**Fah:** She also published "Behind the Screen: Love and Performance in Thailand's GL Industry." It's about the *production crews*. She writes about how crew behavior changes around couples. How camera operators subtly reframe shots. How managers develop specific stress responses.

**Lin:** She writes about managers.

**Fah:** She writes about managers. She identifies *protective patterns*. She tracks the way production staff unconsciously coordinate to create privacy for talent they're shielding.

**Lin:** So she's going to watch us.

**Fah:** She's going to watch us watching them. That's worse.

**Prae:** Deep breaths.

**Fah:** I HAVE BEEN BREATHING DEEPLY FOR A YEAR, P'PRAE. THERE IS NO MORE DEPTH.

Lin locked her phone. Went to the bathroom. Leaned against the tile.

Three months. Ninety days of keeping Jan and JingJing alive despite their best efforts to destroy themselves with the sheer gravitational force of being in love.

Her phone buzzed one more time.

**Fah:** UPDATE. Jan just kissed JingJing's forehead in front of the entire production office. At her DESK. The journalist isn't even here yet. If she does this when Sirin ISN'T here, what is she going to do when she IS?

Lin closed her eyes.

It was going to be a very long three months.
