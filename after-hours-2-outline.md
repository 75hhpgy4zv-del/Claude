# After Hours 2: The Double Wedding

## Series Outline & Story Bible

### PREMISE

The network has greenlit a special finale event for *Enemies with Benefits: After the Fall* — a two-part wedding episode that will air live, with a behind-the-scenes documentary crew embedded on set for the full production. The documentary will follow Jan and JingJing through rehearsals, costume fittings, table reads, and the live broadcast itself. It's a prestige project. A cultural event. The kind of thing that trends nationally.

The journalist assigned to the embedded documentary crew is **Sirin**.

Sirin has published two well-regarded long-form pieces on real relationships behind GL shows — thoughtful, nuanced work that treats its subjects with respect but does not flinch from the truth. She's not a gossip columnist. She's a *journalist*. She asks good questions. She notices things. She writes with precision and empathy, which makes her more dangerous than a tabloid hack, because her work is credible.

Sirin is on set every day. In the production office. In the hallways. Near the dressing room. At the craft services table. She has a press lanyard and a notebook and a gentle, disarming smile, and she is *everywhere*.

Jan and JingJing had just gotten comfortable. The crew knew. Fah was their gatekeeper. The office had a betting pool. They held hands in the hallway. They stopped arriving separately.

Now they have to put all of it back in the box.

The set — their safe space, the place they'd finally been able to be themselves — becomes the most dangerous place in the world. They have to hide *harder* than they did before anyone knew, because now there are sixty crew members who all know the truth and have to pretend they don't, and one journalist who doesn't know and is professionally trained to find out.

Meanwhile: they are planning a real, secret wedding. While rehearsing a fictional one. While Sirin watches.

### THE IRONY ENGINE

The show's wedding arc requires Jan and JingJing to:
- Rehearse vows (while writing real ones in private)
- Do costume fittings for wedding dresses (while secretly shopping for real ones)
- Answer interview questions about "what marriage means to your character" (while knowing what it means to *them*)
- Film love scenes with increasing intimacy for the finale (while having real sex in the same building)
- Attend press junkets where reporters ask "are you two really together?" and they say "we're just very good friends" while wearing matching rings they have to take off for cameras

Every professional obligation is a funhouse mirror of their private reality. Every lie they tell in public is the truth with the serial numbers filed off.

### SIRIN

- Early 30s. Former features writer for a major Thai publication, now freelance
- Has published "Behind the Screen: Love and Performance in Thailand's GL Industry" — a respected, award-nominated piece
- Has published "The Ship and the Shore: When Fan Fiction Becomes Fact" — about the pressure GL actresses face to perform queerness for fans while navigating their actual identities
- She is bisexual herself. This is not widely known. It makes her coverage more personal, more careful, and more insightful than the average entertainment reporter
- She is NOT trying to out anyone. She's trying to write a good documentary. But she's observant and she's smart and the truth has a way of showing itself to people who are paying attention
- Her presence forces the crew into a conspiracy of silence — sixty people who all know and must all pretend they don't
- She picks up on the *quality* of the pretending. People who genuinely don't know something behave differently from people who are actively hiding something they know. Sirin can tell the difference.

### JAN'S EXHIBITIONISM

Post-engagement, Jan's careful control has developed a crack — and the crack runs in a specific direction. She doesn't just want JingJing. She wants to want JingJing *where someone could see*. The proximity of exposure is the accelerant. The risk is the turn-on.

This was always latent. The shower with the window. The dressing room with the thin walls. The balcony in Chiang Mai. But now it's conscious. Jan *knows* she likes it, and the knowledge makes it worse — makes her seek it out, engineer situations, push boundaries.

This creates a direct collision with the stakes: every risky encounter is another moment Sirin could catch. Jan's kink and the plot's threat are the same thing. The thing that turns her on is the thing that could destroy them.

JingJing is complicit. JingJing is *enthusiastically* complicit, because JingJing has never met a bad idea she didn't want to ride until the wheels came off. But JingJing is also scared — a fear she buries under bravado and bratting — because the exposure Jan craves isn't theoretical anymore. There's a journalist with a notebook twenty feet away.

### THE PR SUBPLOT

The network's PR team, headed by a woman named **Khun Dao**, has a vision for the finale's marketing. That vision includes:
- Jan doing a magazine cover with a male model ("just for optics, we're not saying anything, it's just a *vibe*")
- JingJing being positioned as "single and focused on her career" in interviews
- Both of them participating in fan service events where they flirt with fans and each other in carefully choreographed ways that suggest possibility without confirming reality
- A strict social media strategy that controls what they post, when, and with whom

Khun Dao is not a villain. She's a pragmatist. She knows what sells, she knows what the conservative sponsors want, and she knows that two female leads in a confirmed real relationship would be either the biggest story of the year or the end of the show's commercial viability, depending on which way the wind blows.

The tension: Jan and JingJing have to perform being single while being engaged, perform flirting while being in love, perform the *possibility* of their relationship while living its *certainty*.

### SUPPORTING CAST

**Fah** — Now promoted to senior PA. The gatekeeper. The only person on the crew who knows about both the engagement AND the secret wedding. She runs interference with Sirin like a secret agent. She is extremely stressed and extremely dedicated and she deserves a raise.

**Lin** — The third lead. Knows about the relationship (Chiang Mai balcony). Doesn't know about the engagement or the wedding. A natural ally but an unknowing one.

**Prae** — The director. Knows about the relationship. Fiercely protective of her cast. Will body-block Sirin if necessary. But also needs the documentary to go well for the show.

**Mook** — Has moved on gracefully. Becomes an unexpected ally — she deflects Sirin's questions about Jan and JingJing with the practiced ease of someone who has nothing to hide, because she doesn't. Her earlier pursuit of Jan actually serves as useful cover: Sirin initially considers Mook as a possible romantic interest for Jan, which buys Jan and JingJing time.

**Khun Dao** — PR head. Not a villain. A machine. Sees people as assets and relationships as liabilities. Will be the last person to accept the truth and the first person to figure out how to monetize it.

**Noo-Noo** — The stuffed rabbit. Still here. Still important. JingJing can't bring her to set anymore because Sirin would notice and it's "not on brand." JingJing is quietly devastated by this.

### CHAPTER STRUCTURE (Target: 75,000+ words, ~30 chapters)

**ACT 1: THE SAFE SPACE COLLAPSES (Ch 1-7)**

1. **The Announcement** — The finale wedding arc is announced. The documentary crew is introduced. Sirin arrives on set. Jan and JingJing realize they have to go back in the box. (~3,000 words)

2. **Day One** — First day with Sirin on set. The crew's conspiracy of silence begins. Everything that was easy — the hand-holding, the coffee, the lower-back touches — becomes loaded again. JingJing can't bring Noo-Noo. (~3,000 words)

3. **The Interview** — Sirin's first sit-down with Jan and JingJing. Separate interviews. Careful questions about chemistry, working relationships, personal lives. JingJing lies smoothly. Jan lies badly. (~2,500 words)

4. **The Fitting** — Wedding dress fittings for the show. JingJing sees Jan in a wedding dress for the first time and has to pretend it doesn't wreck her. That night: the real wedding planning begins in secret. Forehead kisses. Noo-Noo. Sex that's slow and desperate and full of "I want to marry you and I can't tell anyone." (~4,000 words)

5. **The First Risk** — Jan's exhibitionism surfaces. A stolen kiss in a hallway she *knows* Sirin might walk down. JingJing is terrified and turned on. They fight about it. The fight becomes something else. (~3,500 words)

6. **The Instagram Problem** — Khun Dao presents the social media strategy. Jan is asked to do the magazine cover with the male model. JingJing's reaction. The public performance of singleness begins. (~2,500 words)

7. **The Fan Meet** — A public appearance where fans ask if they're together. The practiced deflection. The screaming fans who ship them. The surreal experience of performing the possibility of the thing that's already real. That night: possessive, intense sex. JingJing reclaiming Jan after watching her perform availability all day. (~4,000 words)

**ACT 2: THE TIGHTENING (Ch 8-16)**

8. **Sirin Notices** — Sirin observes the crew's behavior. They're *too* careful. Too coordinated in their non-reactions. She starts pulling at the thread. (~2,500 words)

9. **The Rehearsal** — Table read of the vow scene. Jan and JingJing read fictional vows to each other in a room full of people and a journalist with a notebook. The emotional weight nearly breaks them. That night: they write their real vows, separately, in different rooms. (~3,000 words)

10. **The Close Call** — Sirin finds the dressing room locked during a break. Hears something she can't identify through the door. Fah runs interference. The near-miss recalibrates their risk tolerance — for about twelve hours. (~3,500 words)

11. **The Magazine** — Jan's cover shoot with the male model. JingJing watches from the sidelines. The photographs. The poses. The forced intimacy. JingJing's jealousy isn't the cute kind anymore — it's the kind that has teeth. That night: violent, possessive sex. JingJing marks Jan everywhere the photographer won't look. (~4,000 words)

12. **The Article Draft** — Sirin shows Prae an early draft of her documentary piece. It's perceptive. It doesn't name what's between Jan and JingJing, but it circles it — describes the way they orbit each other, the way the crew moves around them like planets around a binary star. Prae warns Jan. (~2,500 words)

13. **The Secret Venue** — Jan and JingJing visit the venue for their real wedding. A small garden in Nonthaburi. Twelve guests. No press. They taste cake. They choose flowers. JingJing cries. Jan holds her. It's the happiest day of their lives so far, and no one can know about it. (~3,000 words)

14. **Noo-Noo** — JingJing smuggles Noo-Noo to set in defiance of the brand strategy. Sirin sees the rabbit. Asks about it. JingJing has to lie about something that is genuinely, deeply, personally sacred to her. It costs her. Jan finds her crying in the bathroom after and holds her until the shaking stops. That night: the softest sex they've ever had. All forehead kisses and whispered names. (~3,500 words)

15. **The Photo** — Sirin has a photo. Not from her own camera — from a fan account. A slightly blurry image from outside the Chiang Mai hotel. Two figures on a second-floor balcony. The image is ambiguous but suggestive. Sirin asks Jan about Chiang Mai. Jan's poker face holds. Barely. (~3,000 words)

16. **The Trap** — Jan engineers an encounter. Deliberate. Calculated. She wants to be *almost* caught. She wants the thrill of Sirin being ten feet away while she has JingJing pressed against a wall. JingJing calls her on it. The fight is real this time. The makeup sex is devastating. (~4,500 words)

**ACT 3: THE CRISIS (Ch 17-23)**

17. **Sirin Knows** — Sirin doesn't have proof, but she has a thesis. She sits Jan down for an off-the-record conversation. "I'm not trying to out you. I'm trying to write something true." Jan neither confirms nor denies. The conversation haunts her. (~3,000 words)

18. **The Network** — Khun Dao finds out about the engagement — not from Sirin, but from the ring. JingJing forgets to take it off before a press photo. Khun Dao sees it, recognizes it, confronts them. The meeting is brutal. Khun Dao presents two options: deny everything, or let the network control the reveal for maximum ratings impact. Jan and JingJing want a third option: telling the truth, on their own terms. (~3,500 words)

19. **The Ultimatum** — The network pressures them. The secret wedding is two weeks away. The show's finale is three weeks away. Everything is converging. Jan and JingJing fight — not with each other, but with the machine that wants to own their story. (~3,000 words)

20. **The Vow Scene** — Filming the show's wedding. Take after take of fictional vows. The emotional bleed between performance and reality becomes total. On the last take, Jan goes off-script. She says something real. The director keeps rolling. The crew is silent. Sirin is watching. (~4,000 words)

21. **The Decision** — Jan and JingJing decide: they will get married in secret, as planned. They will not let the network control the reveal. They will not let Sirin break the story. They will tell the truth themselves, when they're ready, how they're ready. Fah is tasked with making this happen. Fah is stressed. (~2,500 words)

22. **The Secret Wedding** — The real ceremony. Twelve people. A garden. Sunset. Noo-Noo as ring bearer (Fah's idea). Real vows. Real tears. Real forever. The sex that night is transcendent — the first time as wives. (~5,000 words)

23. **The Morning After** — Waking up married. The ring on the finger. The world unchanged and completely different. Going to set the next day. Pretending nothing happened. The hardest performance of their lives. (~2,500 words)

**ACT 4: THE REVEAL (Ch 24-30)**

24. **The Finale** — The live broadcast of the show's wedding episode. Jan and JingJing perform their characters' wedding in front of millions. The irony is total. They are already married. No one watching knows this. (~3,000 words)

25. **The Wrap Party** — The show is done. The documentary is in post-production. Sirin submits her final draft. It's beautiful, perceptive, and stops just short of the truth — not because she doesn't know, but because she's chosen not to write what isn't hers to tell. (~3,000 words)

26. **The Post** — Jan and JingJing go public. An Instagram post. Simple. A photo of their hands — two rings — with a caption that says everything and nothing. The internet breaks. (~2,500 words)

27. **The Fallout** — The reaction. The fans. The press. The network scrambling. Khun Dao pivoting to spin. The crew celebrating. Fah crying. Mook sending flowers. The world knowing what the world was never supposed to know, and it being okay. It being more than okay. (~3,000 words)

28. **Sirin's Piece** — Sirin publishes. Not an exposé. Not an outing. A love letter to the space between performance and truth, fiction and reality, the show and the life. She quotes Jan: "I never hated her. I wanted her so badly it felt like hate." She doesn't say where the quote came from. She doesn't have to. (~2,500 words)

29. **The Honeymoon** — They leave. Finally. Together. Publicly. No separate arrivals. No pretending. JingJing brings Noo-Noo to the airport and doesn't care who sees. The sex on the honeymoon is every kind they've ever had — rough, tender, risky, slow — and one new kind: free. (~4,000 words)

30. **Epilogue: One Year Later** — Jan and JingJing on set. A new show. A new story. Same dressing room. Same lock on the door. Same JingJing, annoying Jan until Jan does something about it. Same Jan, pretending to be annoyed while already unbuckling her belt. Noo-Noo on the love seat, facing the wall, seeing nothing, knowing everything. (~2,500 words)

**Estimated total: ~95,000 words**

### THEMES

- **The double life as performance** — they are actresses performing fiction while living a truth they can't speak. The show is a mirror. The mirror is cracked.
- **Exhibition as vulnerability** — Jan's kink isn't about showing off. It's about the terror and the thrill of being *seen* — of letting the mask slip, of wanting the world to know what she has even as she hides it.
- **The cost of the closet** — not a coming-out narrative in the traditional sense, but a story about what it costs to hide something real inside something fake, and what it means to choose your own moment.
- **Tenderness as radical act** — in a world that demands performance, the forehead kiss is revolution. Noo-Noo is revolution. Saying "I'm scared" is revolution.
- **Who owns your story?** — The network wants to own it. Sirin wants to tell it. The fans want to consume it. Jan and JingJing want to *live* it. The question of the book: who gets to decide when and how a love story becomes public?
